How It Works

What’s a “parameter,”and why should you care?

You’ll see AIs described by their number of parameters—ours is a 27-billion-parameter model.  Think of parameters as the connections in the AI’s “brain”: the more it has, the more it can do—but the more computer it takes to run.

The free and paid chatbots online use enormous models—hundreds of billions, even trillions of parameters—running on rows of specialized computers that cost more than a house.  Ours is smaller.  It’s also private, free, offline, and sitting on your desk.  For the things people actually do—write an email, summarize a document, answer a question, read a photo—27 billion is plenty.

The Breakthrough

A big-league AIon a modest machine.

The headline feature of the Marauder is an AI with 27 billion parameters that can also see—read a document, a photo, a form, and answer about it.  A model that size normally needs a graphics card costing thousands of dollars.  We run it on an 8GB card most of the industry wrote off.

It’s built on PrismML’s Bonsai 27B—a 2026 breakthrough that fits a full-size AI onto an everyday computer.  The kind of model that used to need a data center now runs on your desk.  And the same technology powers both sizes of our assistant: it’s why even the Coffee Lake Gamer’s 6GB card can run a real, private, offline assistant—and why the Marauder can run the bigger one that sees.  Two things make it work.

🧱  We shrink the AI without dumbing it down.
The PrismML Compression Breakthrough
27B → ~6GB▼

A technology called PrismML is the key.  It compresses the AI down to a fraction of its normal size—squeezing a 27-billion-parameter model to about 6GB—without gutting what it knows.  That’s the difference between “needs a $2,000 card” and “runs on the 8GB card in your Marauder.”  It’s the same breakthrough that lets us hand you an AI this capable on a refurbished machine instead of a workstation that costs five times as much.

👁️  We let the whole machine help it “see.”
How Vision Works On An 8GB Card
Teamwork▼

Looking at an image is a separate, heavy job on top of thinking about it.  Instead of cramming both onto the graphics card, we hand the “looking” part to the rest of the computer—the processor and memory do the seeing while the graphics card does the thinking.  That teamwork is the whole trick, and it’s why the Marauder’s twelve cores and full 32GB matter: they’re what let an 8GB card read a picture at all.  In our own testing, the AI read and analyzed a complex, dense document in about 45 seconds.
