First — The $849 Trap

Peak FPS pads the stats.Sustained FPS wins the day.

The cheapest new gaming PC this Black Friday is an $849 box—a fast graphics card bolted to 16GB of memory on a single stick, running a bloated, Copilot-laden Windows.  On the spec sheet it looks great.  But the number you actually feel is the sustained frame rate—what the machine still holds with a browser, a voice chat, and the game all running at once.  Same price, same 1080p game, the whole machine working:

Sustained while multitasking — what you actually feel Advertised / peak — the spec-sheet number

The expensive card isn’t the problem.  16GB on a single channel and a bloated, Copilot-laden Windows drag the OEM box down to a jittery 30–120 the moment real life shows up.  The CL Gamer’s humbler card—with 32GB on two channels and a clean OS behind it—just holds 95 FPS, steady.  So we built the opposite of that box — meet the two machines ↓  Or see the full head-to-head →

The RTX 5060 is the faster card in isolation; the point is the delivered, sustained experience, not a raw-GPU win.  The GTX 1660, 1660 Super, and 1660 Ti play this essentially identically—90 FPS feels the same as 105 to the player.
